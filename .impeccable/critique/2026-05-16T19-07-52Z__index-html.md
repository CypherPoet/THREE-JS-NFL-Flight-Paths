## Anti-Patterns Verdict — **Not slop**

This is one of the rare frontend pieces that has consciously and successfully resisted the default LLM aesthetic.

**Tells absent**: no reflex-reject fonts (Big Shoulders Display + Geist + Departure Mono, self-hosted at `styles.css:1–39`); no gradient text; no glassmorphism in chrome (the comment at `styles.css:506` actively polices it); no hero-metric template; no side-stripe borders; no identical card grid. The warm-graphite palette explicitly rejects the predicted first-order category reflex — see the comment at `styles.css:60`: "Sunday Night Football primetime broadcast … warm-graphite tunnel axis, not cool-navy."

**Lane landed in**: editorial-cartographic. Cousin of NYT Upshot / Reuters Graphics, with industrial-display type as voice. Credible, defended, not in the currently-saturated editorial-typographic reflex lane.

**Where slop pressure leaks**:
- The four-icon topbar rail (mileage / methodology / share / theme) is universal SaaS-utility grammar. Generic.
- Both modals (mileage breakdown, methodology) are "modal-first thinking lite." Neither needed a scrim+blur to do its job.

**Deterministic scan**: `npx impeccable detect --json index.html` returned `[]`. Consistent with the holistic read. *(Caveat: CLI scanned markup only; the deeper UI lives in `src/*.js` + `styles.css`.)*

## Overall Impression

The globe is genuinely the hero. The warm paper-on-graphite palette is the strongest single design decision here — it forces the cool sectional sphere to read as a *separate object* rather than tone-matching the chrome. Typography commits (the H1 is the only fluid step; everything else stays on fixed rungs — disciplined). The per-team inline mileage bar with international-segment overlay is FT-Visual-Journalism quality, and the methodology disclosure builds the trust that makes the headline number ("585,478 mi") believable.

The single biggest opportunity: **the headline finding doesn't get headline typography**. "League total miles · 585,478" lives as a `--text-base` mono chip identical to the international metric next to it. The number is the punchline of the whole piece, and it's set at the same size as the supporting figure.

## What's Working

1. **Typographic system** (`styles.css:74–95`, `:493–502`). Three self-hosted families with distinct jobs: Big Shoulders Display carries the H1, Geist runs the UI, Departure Mono carries every number. The comment at line 491 ("Big Shoulders condenses too tightly at 13px to carry the kicker grammar") is the kind of multi-size evaluation most portfolios skip — and it points at a real, considered tradeoff rather than a reflex.

2. **Per-team inline mileage bar** (`styles.css:830–866`). Single row carries team name + division + total miles + a 3px bar where the team accent fills the total length and `--intl-marker` overlays the international portion. Three data points, one row, no chart noise. The `color-mix` shadow that ghosts the team accent behind the international segment (`styles.css:860`) is precisely the right amount of detail.

3. **Stadium tooltip empty-state reticle** (around `styles.css:1065`). When Wikipedia returns 404 for a stadium photo, the fallback uses `mask-image` so a reticle SVG inherits theme color. That's the kind of error state most portfolios just hide.

## Priority Issues

### [P1] The headline number doesn't get headline typography
**What**: "League total miles" displays a six-figure number ("585,478") at `var(--text-base)` (16px) in mono, identical in weight to the supporting "international miles" figure next to it. The eye has no reason to land there first.
**Why it matters**: This is a portfolio piece whose entire point is *"look how absurd NFL travel is."* Burying the punchline as a utility metric reads as risk-aversion. The H1 ("NFL Flight Paths") is the only display-typographic moment in the chrome, but it's the wordmark, not the finding.
**Fix**: Promote the league total to display type (something on the Big Shoulders ladder), pair it with a smaller, looser caption ("across 272 regular-season games · 2026"). Demote the international figure or move it into the panel kicker. Two metric tiles side-by-side competing for the same hierarchy is the lazy answer.
**Suggested command**: `/impeccable typeset` or `/impeccable bolder`.

### [P1] Hint strip is doing onboarding's job
**What**: `index.html:336–338` — "Drag to rotate · scroll to zoom · hover a stadium · click a team to toggle · shift-click to isolate." Five chained instructions in always-on `--text-micro`, `pointer-events: none`. Worse: on mobile the entire hint + legend are hidden (`styles.css:1282–1285`), so mobile users get zero discoverability for the panel interactions.
**Why it matters**: Five different interactions packed into ambient text means users either read it (cognitive load) or miss it (recall failure). The mobile drop is the worse failure — first-timers on phones never learn the panel is interactive.
**Fix**: One-time first-visit overlay (dismissable, with a small `?` re-summon pill near the icon rail). On mobile, surface the same overlay on first sheet-up. The bottom-of-screen real estate currently spent on the always-on strip can become a single subtle "what is this?" affordance.
**Suggested command**: `/impeccable onboard`.

### [P2] Topbar over-clusters on desktop
**What**: `index.html:35–242` — the desktop topbar carries (1) brand mark + H1 + sublabel + season switch in one `topbar__brand` cluster, then (2) two metric tiles, then (3) a four-icon rail. The season switch nested inside the brand cluster forces "title → subtitle → toggle" to read as one thing; the existing mobile duplicate (`index.html:79–83`) is the team admitting it's wrong on small screens.
**Why it matters**: Three layers of grouping in a single horizontal strip is the topbar version of a wall of cards. The season switch in particular is a *data-scope* control — pairing it with the metrics it scopes (instead of with the wordmark) makes the relationship visible.
**Fix**: Pull the season switch out of `topbar__brand`. Pair it with the metric tiles as a "data scope" group. Brand cluster becomes mark + titles only.
**Suggested command**: `/impeccable layout` or `/impeccable shape topbar`.

### [P2] Two scrim modals where one inline view would do
**What**: `index.html:380–408` (mileage) and `index.html:411–522` (methodology). Both use blurred scrims and centered cards. The mileage modal lists per-team miles — but those bars *already exist* in the team panel as the primary interaction. The methodology modal contains five headings of dense prose that wants to be readable, not chrome-burst.
**Why it matters**: Modal-first is the absolute-ban "modal as first thought." Mileage modal duplicates work. Methodology modal is essentially a side-essay shoved into a 640×80vh scrim card.
**Fix**: Remove the mileage modal entirely (the panel bars are the answer). Move methodology into a slide-in side panel from the right, or a routed `?methodology` view that the share-link control could also point to. The icon-rail button collapses from two to one.
**Suggested command**: `/impeccable distill` (remove mileage modal) + `/impeccable shape methodology-view`.

### [P3] Icon rail is the most generic surface
**What**: Four buttons (mileage, methodology, share-link, theme-toggle) sit in a top-right rail. Universal SaaS-utility grammar; the one place a viewer could say "I've seen this rail." After P2 removes the mileage button, three remain — but share is one-time, methodology is once-per-visit, theme is the only persistent control.
**Why it matters**: Doesn't break anything, but it's the surface most exposed to the "AI made that" reaction. Brand register asks for distinctiveness everywhere.
**Fix**: Collapse to (a) theme toggle as a persistent control, (b) a single overflow affordance (`···` or a labelled "more") for methodology and share. Or commit the other direction — give each button a tracked-uppercase mono label below it, lean into the field-instrument register the rest of the design already inhabits.
**Suggested command**: `/impeccable distill` or `/impeccable delight`.

### [P3] "Buy me a coffee" link is incongruous in the legend
**What**: `index.html:345–372`. The bottom-right legend cluster holds two semantic items (home stadium dot, away venue dot) and then a third item that is none of those — a coffee link. Reads as a third legend entry until you look at the icon.
**Why it matters**: It's not slop, but it dilutes the legend's job. The legend is currently the one place that explicitly teaches a viewer to read the globe.
**Fix**: Separate the support link into its own slot — paired with attribution / "made by" metadata in a discrete corner, or moved into the future methodology side-panel.
**Suggested command**: `/impeccable layout`.

## Persona Red Flags

**NFL fan on mobile, from social** (primary audience for a portfolio piece like this):
- Hint strip + legend hidden below 760px (`styles.css:1282–1284`).
- The 30%-height peek-up team panel has no visual affordance that says "swipe up."
- Topbar metrics drop on mobile (`styles.css:1279–1280`) and the league total reappears as a kicker inside the panel — but only if the user opens the panel.
- Risk: never discovers the panel is interactive, never sees the headline number, leaves.

**Hiring manager evaluating frontend craft** (secondary audience but probably the actual conversion target):
- Will open DevTools. Will find the CSS comments at `:60`, `:157`, `:493`, `:506` — these read like a senior designer narrating decisions. Net strong positive.
- Will pause on the icon-rail genericness.
- Will notice the per-team mileage bar uses `color-mix` and CSS custom properties for per-team accent injection. Quietly impressive.

**Sports-data analyst** (the methodology audience):
- Opens methodology first.
- Will appreciate the WGS-84 vs sphere disclosure (`index.html:452`) and the explicit "what's not modelled" section (`index.html:501`).
- Will want a CSV / JSON export of the per-team totals. Won't find one.
- Methodology disclosure builds the trust that makes the per-team numbers credible.

## Minor Observations

- `.hint` and `.legend` `text-shadow: 0 1px 2px var(--shadow)` (`styles.css:884`, `:898`) does almost nothing against deep blacks in dark mode. Either swap for a `paper-soft` scrim behind the text or drop the shadow on the dark theme.
- `data-active="false"` opacity (around 0.36 / 0.5) for inactive season-switch and team-row bars is borderline for AA contrast on the warm-grey background. Worth a contrast check.
- Mileage-modal plane-fly animation (around `styles.css:1678`) is the project's signature motion moment — and it's locked behind a modal a user has to open. Promote that motion to the inline team-panel bars as a one-time entrance, and the project gets a peak-moment for free.
- `metric__sub` "— games" placeholder text (`index.html:115`) reads as un-replaced template until JS fills it. Skeleton it or render it empty.
- Mileage modal titled "Team travel miles" / topbar labelled "League total miles" — pick one terminology.
- Class name `panel__head-meta__league` is awkward double-BEM; not breaking, worth a rename in passing.
- `font-synthesis: none` (`styles.css:89`) is correct given the declared variable-weight ranges, but pair it with at least one comment somewhere — future-you will inherit unfamiliar weight ranges and not know why a font fell back.
- The season-switch active pill uses solid `--ink` background (`styles.css:220`). Strong contrast, but on dark mode that's a near-cream button — a touch loud against the warm-graphite chrome. Consider a softer active treatment in dark mode specifically.
- The `loading__bar` keeps animating under `prefers-reduced-motion` with a justified comment (`styles.css:1311–1316`). Correct call; preserve.
- Favicon and apple-touch-icon hrefs (`index.html:19–23`) are root-relative. Vite rewrites these with the configured `base` ("/THREE-JS-NFL-Flight-Paths/") on Pages — verified pattern, but worth a sanity-check in `dist/index.html` next deploy.

## Questions to Consider

1. **Should the team panel exist at all?** It does two jobs (filter the arcs + read mileage). Both could live on the canvas — hover-to-name, click-to-isolate, with mileage revealed on click. A globe-as-index would be more confident than a globe-with-sidebar.
2. **Where is the peak moment?** "585,478 miles" is the finding, but it's not the peak — the peak is the first moment a user sees the dense arc-tangle from the default angle. Should the headline number animate into being once the arcs land, instead of being there at first paint?
3. **Why two seasons?** The 2025 ↔ 2026 toggle works, but never tells me *why I'd compare them*. A delta ("+3.2% vs 2025", or one specific team that swung hardest) would make the switch a *finding* rather than a *feature*.
4. **What's the field-instrument version of the icon rail?** Three families and a warm-graphite palette earn the right to costume the controls. Tracked-uppercase mono labels under each icon, or a single ledger-card column on the right edge listing every control with hotkey hints?
